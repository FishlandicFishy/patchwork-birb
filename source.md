# Summaries of Source Works

## Introduction
This document serves to summarise the gameplay of a portion of works inspiring `patchworkBirb`. These works are not accessible online, and referencing them in `patchworkBirb` without reasonable attribution would be meaningless. This summary aims to provide context for viewers unfamiliar with the original works while ensuring that the relevant documentation is not lost to time.

## Disclaimer
Fishy does NOT own any of the works below, except for *Struck by the 'Tism* which Fishy only partially owns.

## *Escape School Obby No Clickbait*
*Escape School Obby No Clickbait* is a survival-based tabletop game debuted in the *Become a Board Game Designer* programme. Its main objective is to escape a school environment as quickly as possible (by reaching the "Exit" card with a key) with five students trapped in the school.

It is described as a competitive game with turn-based movement, where the fastest to escape wins. "Events" may happen.

### Gameplay

The map is initially represented by 25 "map" cards. As each player moves across the map, explored map cards are flipped to reveal their locations (e.g. classroom).

### Details
* Players: 5
* Time: around 30 minutes
* Target audience: high schoolers

### Player Stats
* ATK (Attack)
* DEF (Defense)
* Healing
* Buff

### Cards
#### Event Cards (~9)
* Assembly: By the next round, all players must be on the same tile; those who are not on the same tile lose one heart.
* *monsuta* Incoming: By the next round, all players must be on any corner tile; those who are not on a corner tile lose one heart.
* Virus: A player is voted to have a "virus"; in the next three rounds, those who are on the same tile as the "infected" player lose one heart.
* Thirty-Dollar Chicken (?): All players must discard one card.
* Pointing: All players must point towards a player of choice. The player who is pointed at the most loses one heart.
* Maze: All maze cards move to the left; maze cards on the leftmost column move to the rightmost column.
* Cancelled Test: All players gain one heart, except those with full health.
* Your Cards Are Better: One card of each player's choice must be given to the players on their right.
* Odd Numbers: For the next round, players can only move in odd-numbered tiles, or else there will be no effect.

* Draw one item

#### Item Cards (~8)
* Key: can be used to exit the school within 3 rounds; if exceeding 3 rounds, discard
* Trash: useless
  * Permanently borrowed as Trash (37a⁻¹)
* Magnet: if any other player has a key, transfer the key; the key will work for 5 rounds instead of 3
* Ball Pen: attack another player in a 3x3 radius
  * Permanently borrowed as Ball Pen (32a)
* The Kid I Pushed: move any other player forward or backward
* Karate (?): Rock-paper-scissors with any player; loser loses one heart
* Generic Energy Drink: negates negative effects for one round
* The School Nurse's All-Mighty Ice Pack: heal

#### Room Cards (~7):
* monsuta ("monster" in toki pona): -1 heart
* Trap: -1 heart
* General Office: skips one round
* Classroom
* Sofa: +1 heart
  * Permanently borrowed as Sofa (35a)
* Store
  * Permanently borrowed as Store (36a)
* Exit

### Additional Apparatus
* One d6 dice
* Five plastic pawn pieces

## *Goblin* (who says "Ni")
*Goblin* (also known as *Goblin Who Says "Ni"*) is a tabletop game debuted in the *Become a Board Game Designer* programme. According to its creators, the game takes place in "Goblin World" in an "unknown era", and serves to simulate how life is "random", "unfair", and "cruel".

It is unclear whether the premise of the game is related to the Monty Python "knights who say 'ni'".

The nature of the game has directly influenced the card "*jan soweli* who is only 105 cm tall and can only say “ger river”" (39a).

### Details
* Players: 3 to 6
* Time: "Any time"
* Target audience: Kindergarteners (with "complicated" cards removed) to high school students

### Artwork
Artwork is apparently done through "iPad drawings" (possibly on GoodNotes 5).

### Cards
Cards are separated into 15 Upgrade Cards, 5 Downgrade Cards, 40 Action Cards, 10 Reaction Cards, and 10 Trap Cards.

As some of these cards have been lost to time, their respective entries have been omitted.

#### Upgrade Cards (15)
* Upgrade Card (campfire)
* Upgrade Card (firefly in a bottle)
#### Downgrade Cards (5)
* Downgrade Card (cat)
#### Action Cards (40)
* It's time to stop: All action cards ineffective
* The Card's Power: After 2 rounds, if you have no penalty, you will gain one level
* Bye: Tell one player to discard all cards
* Time Machine: Choose any card from the card stack
  * Permanently borrowed as Scavenger (37a)
* Thief: Steal 2 cards from any player
* No Cards?: Tell another player to draw a card
* Frost Spell: A player cannot draw any cards
* The Tall One: The tallest player draws 3 cards
#### Reaction Cards (10)
* Stand Up. Let's Have a Greeting: Slowest to stand up is penalised
  * Permanently borrowed as Stand Up. Let's Have a Greeting (38a)
* Camera: Slowest to pose is penalised
#### Trap Cards (10)
* Detective: If a player looks upward, they lose 3 cards
* Table Span(k?)ing: If a player slaps the table, they lose 3 cards
  * Permanently borrowed as Prosecuted for Damaging Property (1t)
* (lost title): If a player looks at the time, they lose 3 cards
  * Permanently borrowed as What’s the Time? (2t)
* (lost title): If a player wears glasses, they lose 3 cards
  * Permanently borrowed as 🤓 (3t)
* (lost title): If a player sleeps or rests in any way, they lose 3 cards
  * Permanently borrowed as You may not sleep now, there are monsters nearby (4t)

## *Struck by the 'Tism*
*Struck by the 'Tism*<sup>[unofficial title]</sup> is a Monopoly-style tabletop game debuted in the *Become a Board Game Designer* programme. It controversially uses third-party proprietary software in its core design (Nintendon't's fursonas), creating non-free blobs in its firmware.

Fishy has notably participated in its development, but deeply regrets it.

The creators' frequent mispronunciation of "abilities" as "abaties" has inspired the card "Abaties of Characters" (42a).
